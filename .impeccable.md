# Impeccable Design Context

## Design Context

### Users

**Dope Wars**: Adults — players who enjoy gritty, high-stakes trading games with an underground street aesthetic. They appreciate tension, dark humor, and the feeling of operating outside the law.

**Candy Wars**: Kids — younger players experiencing the same mechanics wrapped in a bright, safe, candy-entrepreneurship theme. Parents should feel comfortable with the content.

Both audiences share a love of retro arcade games — the pick-up-and-play loop, the number-watching tension, the "one more turn" pull.

### Brand Personality

**Dope Wars**: Underground. Tense. Gritty.
**Candy Wars**: Playful. Bright. Wholesome.

Both share: Retro. Arcade. Snappy.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Dope Wars**:
- Near-black background (`#0a0a0a`), dark surfaces
- Gold/amber accent (`#e8c840`) + orange-red secondary (`#e05c2a`)
- `Bebas Neue` for display/headers — condensed, loud, street-poster energy
- `Share Tech Mono` for body — terminal/hacker feel
- Tight, dense UI — like a pager or burner phone interface
- References: classic terminal UIs, street-art typography, underground zines

**Candy Wars**:
- Deep purple background (`#0f0a1e`) — night market, neon candy shop
- Pink accent (`#ff79c6`) + orange secondary (`#ffb86c`) — candy colors
- `Fredoka One` for display — rounded, bubbly, fun
- `Nunito` for body — friendly and readable
- Same dense game layout but with softer shapes and warmer energy
- References: 90s candy packaging, Saturday morning cartoons, retro arcade cabinets

**Shared aesthetic constraints**:
- Both are single-file HTML games — no build system, no frameworks
- Dense information layout (stats, market prices, inventory all visible at once)
- Mobile-first but functional on desktop
- Retro arcade energy: everything feels tactile, punchy, responsive
- Anti-references: no glassmorphism, no modern SaaS dashboard aesthetic, no Inter font

### Design Principles

1. **Theme coherence over polish** — every design decision should reinforce the game's world. A button in Dope Wars should feel like a street transaction; in Candy Wars, like picking a treat.

2. **Information density is a feature** — these are strategy games. Players need to see cash, debt, inventory, prices, and days remaining simultaneously. Don't hide information to look clean.

3. **Tactile feedback** — every interaction (buy, sell, travel, event) should feel punchy and immediate. Transitions and state changes matter.

4. **Parallel structure** — both games share identical mechanics and layout structure. Design improvements must be mirrored across both files (preserving each theme).

5. **Retro-first** — lean into the arcade/terminal aesthetic. Pixel-sharp borders, condensed type, high contrast. This is not a modern productivity app.

## Donuts Android Game — Design Context

### Users

Young children (ages 5–9). Playing on a parent's or their own tablet/phone. They likely can't read complex menus, are learning to drag with their finger, and get frustrated easily if matching is hard to see. Parents may also glance at the screen — the game should look age-appropriate and non-stressful.

### Brand Personality

Cozy. Cheerful. Friendly.

### Aesthetic Direction

- Warm cream background (Donuts theme default) — like a bakery window
- Bright, saturated piece colors — easy to distinguish at a glance
- Chunky shapes, generous touch targets — no fiddly UI
- Themes (Donuts/Stars/Dinos/Trucks) let kids pick their favorite world
- Canvas-based Android SurfaceView — no XML layouts for game screen
- Anti-references: no dark mode, no competitive/score-pressure UI, no tiny text

### Design Principles

1. **Kids-first touch targets** — every tappable element must be at least 60×60dp. Kids tap imprecisely and get frustrated by missed taps.

2. **Obvious feedback** — chain lines, animations, and hint rings must be impossible to miss. If a child can't tell what they connected, the game fails.

3. **Forgiving defaults** — sandbox mode (no moves/score pressure) is correct. Hint delay of 5s is good; never make kids feel stuck.

4. **Cozy over competitive** — the counter shows "cleared" not "score." This is the right tone. Avoid any UI that implies failure.

5. **Publishable quality** — not a throwaway prototype. Every theme, button, and animation should feel intentional and polished enough for the Play Store.
